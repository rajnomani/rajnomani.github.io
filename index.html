faers-gabapentin-pregabalin

Real public FDA reports

A Python pipeline on one quarter of FDA adverse event reports. It compares gabapentin and pregabalin for respiratory depression, with and without an opioid on the report.

Eight checks pass, including a second independent count and a re-run that gives an identical results file. Gabapentin against pregabalin: ROR 1.63 (95% CI 1.02 to 2.60).

One quarter of spontaneous reports, with no adjustment for age or illness. The opioid and no-opioid results cannot be told apart. A signal is a lead, not proof that a drug is related to an event.

pv-regulatory-lifecycle

Synthetic safety data, fictional drug

Twelve linked documents on a made-up drug: a synthetic safety database, data checks, signal screening, reporting deadlines, a draft periodic safety report, a risk management plan summary, a label change, a letter to prescribers and a dossier tracker.

All 50 planted data problems found. Rash was the only event that met the screening rule, mostly at 25 mg and age 65 and over (PRR 6.96). Where the Indian guidance is silent, the documents say so instead of guessing. One script re-runs everything and confirms identical results.

All data is invented. My checks were written knowing which problems were planted, so a perfect score does not mean they would catch problems nobody planned for. Rash is a signal to review, not proof of cause.

signal-detection-methods

Real public FDA reports

Compares four standard signal detection methods (PRR, ROR, IC and EBGM) on 14 years of FDA adverse event reports, 2012 Q4 to 2026 Q3, with gabapentin and pregabalin as the worked example.

EBGM is the most conservative method: it flags 24.4% of drug-event pairs, against 37.8% for IC, 42.8% for PRR and 46.1% for ROR. The analysis covers 17,730,954 cases and 1,191,181 pairs. Almost every EBGM signal is also flagged by the other three methods.

Reports show reporting disproportion, not incidence or risk. Duplicates and confounding by indication remain possible. The EBGM code is my own and is not validated against the FDA's reference software. The 30-events-per-case cutoff is a judgement call, and changing it changes the results.

ae-verbatim-coder

Synthetic phrases, open HPO terms

A Python tool that suggests standard terms from the Human Phenotype Ontology for messy adverse event wording, such as "felt sick to my stomach". It is tested on made-up phrases against a separate answer key.

On 65 held-out phrases, scored once after the tool was frozen, 50 are exactly right (76.9%). On the 133 phrases used to build it, 110 are (82.7%). 17 independent checks pass and a second full run gives 6 identical files.

The phrases are made up and cover 20 common events, so real reports will be harder. The sets are small and one clinician reviewed the answer key. The ontology was built for rare-disease features, not drug side effects, so everyday wording is poorly covered: only 4 of 15 plain-language phrases were right. A negation after the symptom ("nausea absent") is missed, and some fuzzy matches are wrong ("all fine" matched Small finger). It suggests codes for a person to review and does not use MedDRA.

clinical-tlf-generator

Synthetic trial data

A Python pipeline that builds standard clinical trial outputs from a made-up trial: a demographics table, an adverse event summary table, an adverse event listing and a figure.

39 independent checks recount every number a second way and all pass. A number I changed on purpose was caught (38 of 39), and a second full run gives 9 identical files.

The data is invented, and the made-up drug's extra rash and nausea are built in. With 60 subjects per arm, no significance tests are run. The datasets are ADaM-style and simplified, adverse event names are not coded with MedDRA, and the outputs are HTML, CSV and PNG, not submission formats.

clinical-data-management

Synthetic trial data

What a data management team does on a trial: edit checks, a numbered query log, simplified SDTM-style datasets, a dashboard and a Data Validation Plan.

11 edit checks found all 36 planted errors. 88 of 120 subjects were clean enough to map, and 32 were held back for open queries.

The trial data is synthetic. The datasets are SDTM-style and simplified, not fully conformant. Edit check flags are leads for a query, not proof of an error.

clinical-research-analysis

Published teaching dataset

A survival analysis written up like a paper: analysis plan first, then Kaplan-Meier curves, Cox regression, and a meta-analysis of four published trials.

Women compared with men, adjusted hazard ratio 0.58 (95% CI 0.41 to 0.80). Pooled SGLT2 inhibitor trials: 0.91 (0.85 to 0.97). A flagged assumption check led to a documented follow-up analysis.

The data are observational and old, so hazard ratios show association and not cause. The follow-up analysis was done after seeing the flag and was not adjusted for multiple testing. The meta-analysis covers four well-known trials and is not a systematic review.

drug-adverse-event-explorer

Real public FDA reports

Charts the most reported side effects of a drug, compares two drugs, and calculates a PRR score, a standard way to flag possible safety signals.

Ibuprofen and acetaminophen are compared as percentages of each drug’s own reports, so a drug with more reports does not look worse. The README explains why reports cannot prove causation.

Reports come from whoever chooses to report, so counts are not rates. A high PRR is a lead to investigate and cannot show that a drug causes an event.

clinical-trials-tracker

Real public registry data

A pipeline and dashboard on ClinicalTrials.gov: which trials are recruiting and where, which records look out of date, and how long trials take.

581 diabetes trials with an Indian site. 35 are recruiting now, and 66 records were flagged as possibly out of date. A script writes the findings report from the data.

Registry data is a snapshot from one day. A flagged record is a lead to check, not proof that the entry is wrong.

healthcare-sql-analysis

Synthetic hospital data

Five SQL queries on a made-up hospital database, using JOINs, CASE, CTEs and a window function.

Admissions by department, city and age group all add up to the same 863 admissions.

The hospital is made up and the query set is small. It shows technique, not hospital findings.

These are self-directed learning projects and not work experience. Five use synthetic data, one uses a published teaching dataset and the rest use public registry or FDA data, so no private patient information is involved in any of them.

Research paper

A full paper from my drug safety work, written the way a journal paper is: the question, the methods, the results and the limits.

RESEARCH PAPER · 2024 FAERS Respiratory depression reports with gabapentin versus pregabalin in the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System, 2024 Raj Akil Nomani · 26 Oct 2026

The paper in four points

  • Question: is respiratory depression reported more often with gabapentin than with pregabalin?
  • Data: 1,480,549 public FDA reports from the four quarters of 2024.
  • Result: yes, ROR 1.81 (95% CI 1.37 to 2.39), with and without an opioid on the report.
  • Limit: reports show a pattern, not cause or how often an event happens.
Read online Download PDF Code and results Preprint on Zenodo (DOI)

These are reports, not rates. FAERS can show a pattern of reporting but not cause or how often an event happens. I did not adjust for age, sex, dose or other illnesses, I used one year only, and the groups without an opioid are small. The findings generate hypotheses.

    How I work

    Plan before results

    I write the question and the method before I run the analysis, and I record every change I make afterwards.

    Check against a known answer

    I plant errors and see whether my checks find them, make totals agree across queries, and compare numbers with the original papers.

    State the limits

    Every project says what it cannot show: synthetic data, observational results, flags that are leads and not proof.
